Enrique Garcia is an American mechanical engineering student at Washington University in St. Louis (WashU), focused on aerospace systems, robotics, and high-reliability autonomy. He currently serves as the Avionics & Systems Integration Lead for WashU VTOL, a student-run program developing semi-autonomous vertical takeoff and landing aircraft.

His work spans embedded firmware, control systems, computer vision, and aerodynamic analysis. Garcia's engineering philosophy centers on building the interface between hardware and software through first-principles design, rigorous hardware-in-the-loop testing, and measurable, validated outcomes.

WashU VTOL

Garcia serves as Avionics & Systems Integration Lead for WashU VTOL, a student-built semi-autonomous vertical takeoff and landing aircraft. He architected the full avionics stack, including Pixhawk 6C flight management unit integration, PX4 autopilot configuration, and all wiring and power distribution.

A key contribution was establishing a hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) validation workflow using Gazebo/PX4 SITL, enabling comprehensive autopilot parameter tuning and failure-mode testing before physical flight. Garcia also resolved a persistent I2C noise fault caused by motor wiring interference (see Incident VTOL-01).

WashU Design/Build/Fly

During 2024–2025, Garcia contributed to WashU Design/Build/Fly (DBF), an AIAA-affiliated competition team. His responsibilities covered aerodynamic analysis and payload system design.

Garcia ran iterative XFLR5 panel-method sweeps and ANSYS CFX cross-checks to validate NACA-series wing geometry across the full operating envelope. He also performed 2.5 g structural load analysis that informed spar placement and composite layup decisions.

Sentinel Computer Vision

Sentinel CV is an independent real-time computer vision pipeline for autonomous target tracking and intercept prediction, developed in Winter 2025. The system implements background subtraction, contour-based detection, and a Kalman filter state estimator in C++ using OpenCV, operating at camera frame rate with low-latency output suitable for closed-loop guidance simulation.

MIRO Lab

During Summer 2025, Garcia worked as an undergraduate researcher at the Multiplatform Interactive Robotics Lab (MIRO Lab) at WashU. He developed a wearable sensor fusion pipeline combining IMU, EMG sensors, and a visual tracking camera, producing temporally synchronized motion signatures used in ongoing human–robot interaction research.

Team Projects § 1.0

WashU VTOL

PROJ-T01 ● ACTIVE

I serve as Avionics & Systems Integration Lead for WashU VTOL — a student-built semi-autonomous vertical takeoff and landing aircraft. My job is owning the entire avionics stack: Pixhawk 6C flight management unit, PX4 autopilot, ESC wiring, power distribution, and all sensor integration.

The most impactful thing I built was a hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) validation workflow using Gazebo/PX4 SITL. This let us tune autopilot parameters exhaustively and test failure modes before putting the aircraft in the air — which is exactly the kind of shift-left validation that matters for flight-critical software.

I also tracked down and resolved a persistent I2C noise fault (see Incident VTOL-01) that was causing intermittent sensor dropouts during bench testing. Root cause was motor wiring EMI on unshielded I2C lines; resolution was re-routing, shielding, and pull-up resistor additions.

WashU Design/Build/Fly

PROJ-T02 COMPLETE

During 2024–2025 I was the Aerodynamics & Payload Engineer for WashU's AIAA Design/Build/Fly competition team. My primary responsibility was validating the wing geometry across the full operating envelope before we committed to manufacturing.

I ran iterative XFLR5 panel-method sweeps to generate Cl/Cd polars for a set of NACA-series candidate profiles, then cross-checked the most promising geometries in ANSYS CFX to catch viscous effects XFLR5 misses. I also did a 2.5 g structural load analysis that directly informed spar placement and composite layup — the resulting wing configuration passed all load cases.

Sentinel Computer Vision

PROJ-T03 COMPLETE

Sentinel CV is a real-time computer vision pipeline I built for autonomous target tracking and intercept prediction. The system chains background subtraction, contour-based detection, and a Kalman filter state estimator, implemented in C++ with OpenCV.

It runs at camera frame rate with latency low enough for closed-loop guidance simulation. The Kalman filter handles occlusion and detection dropout gracefully by coasting on predicted state until measurements resume.

MIRO Lab — Undergraduate Researcher

PROJ-T04 COMPLETE

During Summer 2025 I worked at the Multiplatform Interactive Robotics Lab at WashU. I developed a wearable sensor fusion pipeline that combined IMU, electromyography (EMG) sensors, and a visual tracking camera into temporally synchronized motion signatures for human–robot interaction research.

The challenge was getting three modalities — very different sample rates, latencies, and noise profiles — into a single coherent stream. I built the synchronization and calibration pipeline in Python, with probabilistic uncertainty propagation throughout.

Personal Projects § 2.0

Multi-Sensor Flight Navigation (EKF Nav)

PROJ-P01 PERSONAL

A real-time 12-state Extended Kalman Filter fusing IMU, GPS, and optical flow data, running on a Teensy 4.1 (ARM Cortex-M7 at 600 MHz). The filter state covers 3D position, velocity, attitude quaternion, and IMU bias terms.

Covariance gating handles sensor dropout — if a measurement's innovation exceeds a statistical threshold, it's rejected rather than corrupting the state estimate. This makes the system resilient to GPS outages and IMU glitches without a full reset.

C++Teensy 4.1 EKFIMU GPSEmbedded

Hyperion 6-DOF Physics Engine

PROJ-P02 PERSONAL

A high-frequency (10 kHz) rigid-body physics engine for validating flight software. It implements the full 6-DOF translational and rotational equations of motion with Runge-Kutta 4th-order integration, validated against analytical benchmarks for conservation of energy and momentum.

The engine is multithreaded — a physics thread runs the integration loop while a telemetry thread samples state for logging. An incident (HYPERION-01) taught me the hard way about thread contention: competing mutex locks were introducing timing jitter that corrupted the integration step. I resolved it with a double-buffered state handoff.

C++20Multithreading RK46-DOF Dynamics

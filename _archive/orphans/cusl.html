Research Brief - Unmanned Systems

Estimation, Control, and Dynamics for Cooperative Unmanned Systems

Research focus: unmanned vehicle systems with emphasis on UAV development, vision-aided navigation, wind and gust estimation, cooperative sensing, cooperative control, remote sensing, and experimental validation.

Research Direction & Technical Alignment

CUSL is a multi-disciplinary research group focused on the design, development, and testing of teams of unmanned systems for cooperative sensing and cooperative control missions. The work sits at the intersection of vehicle dynamics, state estimation, control theory, sensing, and field validation.

For my flight mechanics and control interests, this is a strong research environment because the technical problems are not purely software and not purely mechanical. They require an integrated understanding of physical vehicle behavior, uncertain sensor measurements, controller design, and how those models perform when placed on real unmanned aerial systems.

  • Dynamics: Study how UAVs respond to wind, gusts, wake effects, vehicle geometry, and mission constraints.
  • Estimation: Support navigation and environmental awareness through filtering, vision-aided navigation, wind estimation, and cooperative sensing methods.
  • Control: Connect models and sensor estimates to control laws for single-vehicle and multi-agent unmanned systems.
  • Validation: Translate simulation and theory into testing workflows for UAV platforms, networked systems, and field research operations.

Core Research Objectives

Autonomous Systems

Develop and validate unmanned systems that connect sensing, onboard computation, decision logic, and control.

Wind / Gust Sensing

Study how UAVs can estimate and respond to wind, gusts, wake effects, and atmospheric disturbances.

Cooperative Estimation

Use multiple vehicles or distributed measurements to improve situational awareness and sensing quality.

Cooperative Control

Design control approaches for teams of unmanned systems operating in shared environments and mission constraints.

FMC at a Glance

Flight mechanics and control addresses the full problem of vehicle motion: how a body moves through a fluid environment under forces and moments, how that motion is sensed and estimated from noisy measurements, and how feedback control laws drive the vehicle toward desired behavior.

The field is mathematically precise and physically grounded. It requires fluency in rigid body dynamics, linear and nonlinear control theory, probability and estimation, and embedded real-time systems. At the applied level, it is the core of how aircraft, spacecraft, and autonomous vehicles are designed to behave reliably under uncertainty.

Core Areas
01 — Dynamics
Flight Dynamics
Equations of motion for rigid bodies in atmosphere and exoatmosphere. Linearization about trim, stability derivatives, longitudinal and lateral-directional modes. The mathematical foundation everything else depends on.
6-DOF  ·  Trim  ·  Stability Modes  ·  Linearization
02 — Control
Control Systems
Classical and modern feedback control design. Root locus, Bode, state-space formulations. LQR, pole placement, robustness analysis. Autopilot architecture and control law implementation on embedded hardware.
State-Space  ·  LQR  ·  Stability Margins  ·  Autopilot
03 — Estimation
Estimation / Sensor Fusion
State estimation under measurement noise and uncertainty. Extended and Unscented Kalman Filters, multi-sensor architectures. Reconstructing full vehicle state in real time from IMU, GPS, and optical data.
EKF  ·  UKF  ·  IMU+GPS  ·  Covariance Gating
04 — Simulation
Simulation / Validation
High-fidelity numerical simulation of vehicle dynamics and control loops. Hardware-in-loop and software-in-loop testing. Building environments accurate enough to trust before physical flight.
HIL  ·  SITL  ·  6-DOF Engine  ·  RK4
05 — Autonomy
Autonomous Systems
Guidance, navigation, and control for unmanned vehicles. Waypoint following, hover stabilization, multi-mode flight management, and closed-loop mission execution without continuous human input.
GNC  ·  UAV  ·  Mission Autonomy  ·  VTOL

Technical Direction
Problems I want to work on
Autonomous flight in uncertain environments
Vehicles that maintain stable, precise flight under wind, sensor noise, and incomplete state information — without continuous operator input.
State estimation under degraded sensing
Reconstructing full vehicle state when sensors fail, disagree, or drop out. Fault-tolerant estimation and graceful degradation in navigation pipelines.
Simulation for validation — closing the sim-to-real gap
Building simulation environments accurate enough to trust. HIL and SITL methodology as a core part of control law development, not an afterthought.
VTOL and transition flight dynamics
Hybrid vehicles transitioning between hover and fixed-wing modes, where plant dynamics shift and control authority redistributes between actuator sets.
Embedded control and flight software
Real-time implementation of estimation and control on constrained embedded hardware — where timing and computational limits are hard constraints, not soft preferences.
What kind of work I want to do

I want to work on systems where the physics of motion and the mathematics of control are both taken seriously — research environments, advanced engineering teams, and organizations building vehicles that need to perform reliably in the real world.

My preference is work that spans theory and implementation: designing a control law, implementing it on embedded hardware, validating it through simulation and test, and understanding the failure modes. Not one step in isolation — the full chain.

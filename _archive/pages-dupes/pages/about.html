About

Systems · Engineering · Steady Work

I am currently pursuing a B.S. in Mechanical Engineering with minors in Robotics and Mechatronics. I may pursue an M.S. in Mechanical or Aerospace Engineering. I am interested in aerospace and autonomous systems, with a focus on hardware integration, test, and controls, and I am most drawn to work where mechanical systems, sensing, controls, and software all have to behave reliably together.

I like reducing problems to constraints first. From there, I focus on making the system clear: define assumptions, tighten interfaces, trace behavior back to data, and build only what can be tested honestly.

I want to become a systems minded engineer who can contribute meaningfully to demanding aerospace and robotics teams through design, integration, validation, and calm decision making under pressure.

Systems Workflow

Click through the way I usually move from problem framing to refinement.

Constraints

Start by identifying what must be true. Weight, power, geometry, timing, sensing, communication, and safety boundaries usually define the real problem before any detailed solution does.

Interfaces

Most systems become clearer once interfaces are explicit. I try to define what talks to what, what assumptions each subsystem is making, and where failure can hide between components.

Test

Before scaling effort, I want a small honest check. A simple bench test, simulation check, logging pass, or hardware integration step can expose bad assumptions early.

Data

Once the system runs, I care about what the logs and measurements actually say. Data keeps the mental model honest and helps separate intuition from reality.

Refine

After that, refinement becomes targeted. Improve the weak interface, simplify the noisy part, tighten the assumption, or redesign the piece that keeps violating expectations.

Engineering Principles

Expand the principles that matter most to how I work.

I try to understand what the system is actually being asked to do before jumping into tools, opinions, or complexity. That usually makes better decisions possible much earlier.

Hidden assumptions create fragile systems. I would rather expose them early, write them down clearly, and let testing decide whether they survive.

Logs, measurements, and behavior matter more than what the design was supposed to do. Staying close to data keeps the work honest and cuts through ego quickly.

Good engineering is not only about solving your own part well. It is also about making interfaces cleaner, documentation clearer, and collaboration easier for everyone else.

What I am aiming for

  • • Contribute to aerospace and robotics teams that turn ambitious goals into testable engineering steps.
  • • Build tools, documentation, and integration workflows that reduce friction for the rest of the team.
  • • Stay close to hardware, telemetry, and validation so design choices stay tied to real system behavior.
  • • Grow into a systems minded engineer who can simplify complexity, stay calm under pressure, and make sound decisions with incomplete information.

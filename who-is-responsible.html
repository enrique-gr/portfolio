Who Is Responsible When an AI Kills?

Technical Writing | Individual | Spring 2026

Overview

ENGR 3100 magazine piece on autonomous weapons and the accountability gap: when a machine makes a lethal call, who answers? The argument sits at the intersection of aerospace systems, machine learning, humanitarian law, and geopolitics.

What I Examined

01 | Technical Research

Sources

Synthesized DARPA reports, UN findings, ICRC policy, and Human Rights Watch material for a broad engineering audience.

02 | Accountability Gap

Argument

How autonomous targeting breaks the legal responsibility chain, and why neural-network opacity makes that gap structural.

03 | Case Studies

Evidence

X-62A VISTA (Sep 2023) and Kargu-2 in Libya as cases where capability outran governance.

04 | Geopolitical Analysis

Policy

Mapped US, China, and Russia positions in treaty talks: builders of autonomy blocking binding rules.

05 | Web Publication

Deliverable

Built a magazine-style web version alongside print: layout, type, and hierarchy.

Scope | ENGR 3100

Technical writing for a public audience. Research synthesis and clear argument, not a legal brief or weapons design study.

Reflection

The engineering problem of autonomy is solvable. The accountability problem is not, at least not yet. Capable systems and responsible systems are different goals, and the gap between them is still widening.

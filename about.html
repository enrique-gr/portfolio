About

Systems · Engineering · Steady Work

I am a senior Mechanical Engineering student at Washington University in St. Louis. My work sits where UAV systems, embedded sensing, controls, and hardware integration meet: building, integrating, and validating systems that have to behave under real constraints.

I am most drawn to problems where mechanical systems, sensing, controls, and software must agree. I reduce them to constraints first, then clarify assumptions, tighten interfaces, and trace behavior back to data. Graduate study in Mechanical or Aerospace Engineering is on the table, centered on aerospace and autonomous systems with a focus on integration, test, and controls.

Engineering Principles

Expand the principles that matter most to how I work.

I try to understand what the system is actually being asked to do before jumping into tools, opinions, or complexity. That usually makes better decisions possible much earlier.

Hidden assumptions create fragile systems. I would rather expose them early, write them down clearly, and let testing decide whether they survive.

Logs, measurements, and behavior matter more than what the design was supposed to do. Staying close to data keeps the work honest and cuts through ego quickly.

Good engineering is not only about solving your own part well. It is also about making interfaces cleaner, documentation clearer, and collaboration easier for everyone else.

What I am aiming for

  • • Contribute to aerospace and robotics teams that turn ambitious goals into testable engineering steps.
  • • Build tools, documentation, and integration workflows that reduce friction for the rest of the team.
  • • Stay close to hardware, telemetry, and validation so design choices stay tied to real system behavior.
  • • Grow into a systems-minded engineer who can simplify complexity, stay calm under pressure, and make sound decisions with incomplete information.
